## 4. Q&A (2 minutes = 3 to 4 questions; under 30 s each, one-line answer first)

**Who's likely to ask what:** Gwénolé asks about mainnet, BNB Chain, gasless and ERC-8004. Jommy and Dhila ask about the business model, adoption and what happens next. YanZero asks about fake sales, risk and solo scope.

**Why testnet, when some teams are on mainnet?** (Gwénolé)
> "Because Kulaya lends money to real people. Deploying the contracts to mainnet is the easy part; lending real money in Indonesia needs a licensed lender of record, and I don't have one yet. So mainnet comes after the licensed partner, not before."

**Business model? Moat?** (decided: not defined yet)
> "Honestly, it's not finalised. Today the flat fee, at most five percent, goes to the lenders' pool and a first-loss reserve, and Kulaya takes nothing yet. I'll design how it earns together with the licensed lending partner after a pilot. What's defensible is the sales record that piles up for every shop, and a safety model that lets a lender trust an AI."

**How is this different from other "on-chain track record" credit projects?**
> "The shop doesn't do anything extra to build its record: it's just every sale it already makes, every day. And repayment comes out of those same sales, so there's no due date to miss."

**Isn't "AI proposes, contract decides" what other agent projects do too?**
> "Yes, and I think that's the right pattern. Mine is applied to lending to people who've never touched crypto, and the attack is public: anyone can try to make it lend a billion rupiah right now."

**Why BNB Chain?** (Gwénolé, almost certainly)
> "Three practical reasons. IDRX, the rupiah stablecoin, already runs on BNB Chain with rupiah on- and off-ramps, and that's exactly what the QRIS bridge needs. The ERC-8004 registry is here, so the AI's reputation sits on the same chain as the loans it made. And fees are low enough that recording a five-thousand-rupiah sale makes sense, with MegaFuel as the way to sponsor gas in production."

*(All three are from the repo's README. Don't quote a fee figure: none was measured.)*

**What happens after the hackathon?**
> "QRIS bridge through a licensed provider, a licensed lender of record through the OJK sandbox, then a small real pilot. **[Add a timeline only if you'll commit to it.]**"

**Do owners need a wallet?**
> "Today, yes, though they never pay gas: a relayer submits what they sign. Next are embedded wallets and BNB Chain's MegaFuel paymaster, so the owner never has to see a wallet."

**Why does this need a blockchain?** (the cut "why on-chain" slide lives here now)
> "Three things a database can't give you. The shop owns its record and any lender can verify it. Repayment is enforced inside the payment, so there's no collector. And the AI has a public track record: it's ERC-8004 agent #2535, rated by real loan outcomes, and the registry rejects self-reviews."

**How is the credit limit set?** (the cut credit-math slide lives here now)
> "Ten percent of verified sales over thirty days, capped by the shop's level: one million rupiah to start, doubling after each repaid loan, up to eight million."

**What stops a shop from taking cash to dodge repayment?** (the sharpest finance question; have it ready)
> "Honestly, a determined cheater can slow it down: take sales in cash, or send a tiny payment from a friend's wallet every couple of weeks, because today the write-off only looks at silence, not progress. That keeps the money locked, but they can't open a second loan, their limit never grows, and a shop that does stop gets written off after fourteen days, publicly, with the reserve absorbing the loss. The fix is a repayment-progress rule with a long outer limit, still with no late fees and no collectors. It's on the post-hackathon list."

**If the contract decides everything, what does the AI actually do?** (Gwénolé or YanZero)
> "The part a contract can't: it decides whether and how much to offer inside the caps, explains the terms in plain Bahasa, answers questions on Telegram, even voice notes, and can flag suspicious sales. A flag can only lower a limit, never raise it. The contract is the safety net, not the product."

**Is a 3% fee cheap? What's the APR?**
> "It's a flat fee, written up front, the same whether repayment takes a week or two months. No compounding, no late fees. Against pinjol, where the cost grows every day you're late, that's the point."

**What stops fake sales?**
> "At least five different customers, max Rp 250.000 counted per customer per day, minimum payment Rp 5.000. It's parameter-based today, so a person with five wallets could still fake a history, but each fake shop is capped at one million rupiah, and a licensed lender's ID checks, which production needs anyway, are what close that gap."

**What if the AI is jailbroken?**
> "Its key can only propose, it holds no funds, the contract has hard caps, and code writes the numbers. That's exactly what the red-team page attacks."

**Who takes the default risk?**
> "Lenders in the pool, protected by a first-loss reserve of twenty percent of every fee, a five-percent-of-pool cap per loan, and a write-off only after fourteen days without sales. No collectors and no late fees, by design."

**Did you build it alone?**
> "Yes, solo. The visual design and video motion graphics came from Claude Design working from my brief, and the voiceover is an ElevenLabs voice."
